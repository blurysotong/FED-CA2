Diversity in the dark

Meet the twilight residents

From Earth's most abundant vertebrate to a colony that acts like one animal, this layer is full of life adapted to near darkness.

01 / Cyclothone

Bristlemouth

At only 1 to 3 inches long, bristlemouths are tiny. Their estimated population reaches hundreds of trillions or even quadrillions, making them the most abundant vertebrates on Earth.

02 / Vitreledonella richardi

Glass octopus

Its transparent body hides it from predators and prey. Only the digestive tract, optic nerves, and eyes are opaque. With its arms included, it can grow to about 45 centimetres long.

Image source ↗

03 / Malacosteus

Stoplight loosejaw

This predatory fish produces rare red bioluminescence. Most deep-sea animals cannot see red light, so the loosejaw can illuminate prey with an almost invisible spotlight.

Image source ↗

04 / Siphonophorae

Siphonophore

A siphonophore is not one body but a colony of genetically identical clones called zooids. Each zooid specialises in swimming, feeding, defence, or reproduction, and together they function as one superorganism.
