The Sunlight Zone

also called the Epipelagic Zone

The Surface Layer stretches from the ocean's surface to about 200 metres deep. It receives enough light for plants and tiny organisms to perform photosynthesis, making it the ocean's most productive and diverse layer.

If you have gone swimming, snorkelling, or taken a boat out to sea, you have already visited it. This is where most marine life lives—and where people interact with the ocean most.

Why life thrives here

Three bright advantages

Every current, wave, and ray helps keep the upper ocean alive.

☀️

Energy from above

Sunlight lets phytoplankton and marine plants photosynthesise, creating the foundation of the entire ocean food chain.

🌊

Always in motion

Waves, tides, and currents constantly spread nutrients and oxygen through the warm upper water.

🐠

Room to flourish

Relatively low pressure and abundant food make this a comfortable home for an extraordinary variety of marine life.
