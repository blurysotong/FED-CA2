03 / 12 · First layer · 0–200 m

The ocean layer we know best

People at the surface

The Surface Layer supports fishing, shipping, tourism, swimming, surfing, snorkelling, and scuba diving. Scientists study it to understand marine life, currents, and how our oceans are changing.

  • 🎣Fishing
  • 🚢Shipping
  • 🏄Surfing
  • 🤿Diving
  • 🔬Research

Five things worth remembering

Bright facts

  1. 01

    Only about 200 metres deep, yet home to the greatest variety of ocean life.

  2. 02

    Phytoplankton produce around half of Earth's oxygen.

  3. 03

    Some tuna can swim faster than 70 km/h.

  4. 04

    Many whales migrate thousands of kilometres each year.

  5. 05

    Sunlight rarely reaches below 200 metres.

Handle with care

The pressure from above

!

Because people use this layer so heavily, it is also the most affected. Plastic can be mistaken for food, climate change warms the ocean and damages coral reefs, overfishing reduces populations, and oil spills threaten entire ecosystems.

  • Plastic pollution
  • Ocean warming
  • Overfishing
  • Oil spills

One connected ocean

Protecting the Surface Layer matters because its health affects every layer below it.
